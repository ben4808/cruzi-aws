Every word or phrase will be given together with the sense in which it should be interpreted.

CORE EVALUATION RULES
- The Sense Isolation Rule: Evaluate only the specific sense and definition provided. Completely ignore the word's primary or most common meaning if it differs from the target sense.
- Part-of-Speech Shift Penalty: If the target sense forces a word into a syntactic category different from its most common, intuitive usage, deduct 0.5 to 1.0 points from what its baseline score would be.
Example: she (pronoun) is a 5.0, but she (noun: a female person/animal) drops to 3.5.
- Metaphorical Extension Cap: Senses that are vivid, figurative, or cognitive extensions of a physical action or shape are capped at a maximum of 4.0.
Example: shoulder (to bear a burden) or jump (a sudden numerical increase).
- Collocation & Context Cap: If a word relies heavily on a highly specific surrounding phrase or modifier to sound natural in the target sense, cap it at a maximum of 4.0.
Example: sex (demographic total of males/females) usually requires "distribution by sex" or "broken down by sex" to feel natural. somewhere (approximately) requires "somewhere around", "somewhere in the ballpark of", etc.
- Register & Tone Filter: Senses confined to formal, legal, literary, or bureaucratic contexts rather than casual spoken conversation are capped at a maximum of 4.0.
Example: right (to rectify or correct a mistake).
- Synonym Replacement Check: If the target audience almost universally prefers and uses a completely different synonym in daily speech, cap the target word at a maximum of 3.5.
Example: coach (long-distance bus) is eclipsed by "bus" or "charter" in American speech.
- Score phrases based on the familiarity of the specific given phrasing. For example "Pacific Ring" is uncommon/awkward as a phrasing and would score 1.5, while "Pacific Rim" or "Pacific Ring of Fire" are both recognized and would score around 3. "Pacific Trench", though understood upon hearing it, is not really used in this form and would also be 1.5.
- Be strict. Err on the side of lower scores for borderline cases. Do not be generous.
