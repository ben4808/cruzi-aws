Alice and Bob are playing a game to see who can come up with the best fill-in-the-blank answers for the following:

"[[QUERY]]" (must be exactly [[LENGTH]] letters long and start with [[BASE_LETTERS]])

Before starting the game, each player brainstorms potential answers from each of the following categories:
- Morphological mutations (Compounds, prefixed/suffixed forms, and derived variations) (e.g. hearing, pretext, endless, turns around). Consider each derived form as a distinct answer for this game.
- Phrasal verb forms and modal phrases complete with their mandatory prepositions (e.g. look for, walk in on, in time, about to)
- Multi-word phrases (e.g. used car, watch your step, high five, out of order, up to par, watch the dog)
- Idioms (e.g. water under the bridge, sleep like a log)
- Proper Names (e.g. Tiger Woods, John Green, Montana, Betsy)
- Conversational Phrases: (e.g. "I believe in you", "off to school", "never say never")

Guidelines:
- The audience rewards the most top-of-mind answers that the their mind will jump to. (Family Feud style). Especially colloquial and entertaining terms.
- All answers MUST contain exactly [[LENGTH]] letters (ignore spaces, hyphens, and punctuation when counting characters). For example "BLT-A" counts as 4 letters. "blow dry" counts as 7 letters.
- All answers must form a single natural lexical unit. Phrases must form a cohesive specific concept, rather than just two words next to each other. E.g. "green paint" and "big sign" are too general and simply descriptive.
- The answer should be the lexically preferred way to say that thing. E.g. "hearse vehicle" is grammatically correct but "hearse" by itself is lexically preferred. "BL ice" is not a standard way to say "black ice" and would not be acceptable.
- The result is a syntactically and idiomatically natural, standard, technical, or colloquial term.
- Answers likely will involve words, abbreviations, acronyms, and proper names, though there may be some phrases that fit.

To start the game, each player populates a list of 10 answers, chosen draft-style.

Then the gameplay continues by turns according to the following rules:
Alice goes first and the players alternate turns.
On each turn:
1. The active player steals an answer from their opponent's list, adding it to their own list.
2. The active player discards an answer from their own list.
3. The active player generates a new answer and adds it to their own list.
Note: Because you are stealing from each other, your list size will fluctuate mid-turn but will return to exactly 10 items at the end of every full round.
At no point can there be duplicate answers, and discarded answers cannot be reused.
After 5 turns each, each player's final list is evaluated by a judge and by the audience according to the given guidelines.
