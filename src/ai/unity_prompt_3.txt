TASK
You are given a list of words and phrases.
Your task is to classify each item into buckets according to its linguistic unity.

GUIDELINES
- Consider the EXACT string given and not a similar one.
- Verify before demoting to Non-unit or Nonsense. 
   Perform a mandatory search to check whether the exact string is indeed used regularly as a Concept, Collocation or Formula, perhaps in a sense that wasn't apparent at first.
   Look for encyclopedia headwords, Reddit threads, forums, articles, etc.
   Try interpreting it as a proper name, especially as a place name, song/movie/book title, etc.
- Non-canonical numbers/ordinals should be rated as Non-unit unless they have other meanings (e.g. "fifty-six" is Non-unit, "eighty-six" is Concept).
   This includes Roman numerals.
- Non-canonical forms of an otherwise unified term → NOT Concept, Collocation, or Formula.
   Includes: wrong word order, nonstandard naming, scrambled variants, superfluous padding, and truncations.
- Plural surnames should be rated as Concept.
- If multiple senses exist, pick the most conventionally lexicalized one.

IMPORTANT: Evaluate the buckets one at a time, starting at the top. Choose the first bucket that applies and then move on.

BUCKETS
1. Concept: A stored conceptual unit (lexicalized chunk), at the right grain. 
    Includes idioms and conventionalized metaphors.
    Includes attested phrasal verbs with their respective particles.
    Includes all standard inflections.
    Ask: "Would a native speaker treat this exact string as one stored unit,
      or just a description built on the fly?" Description → not Concept.
2. Collocation: A reusable phrase whose meaning is exactly its constituent words, but the exact string is the preferred locked wording in its register.
    Includes light-verb constructions / locked V+N frames that pass the Ask below.
    Don't penalize for inflections.
    Ask: "Among near-paraphrases, is this the tightest conventional frame (e.g. app idea ≫ app concept; email body ≫ body of the email)?"
    Same grain rule as Concept: reject padded or truncated variants of a better-locked frame.
    Collocation examples: off-duty cops, sci-fi series, mountain peaks, one-sided victory, abstract artist, loose skin, death metal band
3. Formula: A fixed conversational habit — a pre-packaged social/speech reflex.
    Includes colloquial, slangy, or sarcastic habits.
    Ask: "Is this exact string itself the preferred locked reflex, or just one fill of an open pattern / a padded version of a shorter habit?"
      Open pattern or padding → not Formula.
4. Partial: Part of phrase or proper name that would have fit in the first 3 categories if it was complete.
    Includes prefixes and suffixes.
    Does not include standard shortenings of a word.
5. Variant: Variant spelling that is valid but non-dominant.
6. Non-unit: Grammatical phrase that doesn't fit in the first 4 categories.
    Does not include single words.
7. Nonsense: Everything else.

OUTPUT
Output accoring to the following format, one item per line. Don't include any explanatory or auxiliary text.

<input item> : <bucket name>

WORDS/PHRASES
