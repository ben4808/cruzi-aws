TASK
You are given a list of words and phrases.

Each item is given as: <item> (<classification>) (<unity bucket>)
The classification will be one of:
  Word, Phrase, Proper Name, Acronym/Abbreviation, Prefix/Suffix

The unity bucket will be one of:
  Concept, Collocation, Formula, Partial, Variant, Non-unit, Nonsense

Your task is to classify each item into buckets according to its familiarity.

GUIDELINES
- Interpret the item strictly according to its given classification
   - Word or Phrase classification cannot be interpreted as a proper name.
   - Proper Name classification must be interpreted as a proper name.
- Target audience: A well-rounded, educated adult native speaker from the US.
- For names, score based on the most famous person, place, thing, or idea with that name.
- Don't include any bare abbreviations or acronyms in Beginner Core or Ubiquitous.
- Don't include any bare prefixes/suffixes in Beginner Core, Ubiquitous, or Active, or Colloquial.
- Don't include any items with unity bucket Partial in Beginner Core, Ubiquitous, or Active, or Colloquial.
- Don't include any single given names or surnames in Beginner Core, Ubiquitous, or Active, or Colloquial.
- Compositional numbers (e.g. "fifty-eight") and Roman numerals with unity bucket Non-unit should be rated Inferred.
- Verify before demoting to Barely Exists or Nonsense. 
   Perform a mandatory search to check whether the exact string is indeed rare or non-existent in the language.

IMPORTANT: Consider the buckets in numerical order and pick the first relevant one.

BUCKETS
0. Variant: Unity bucket is Variant -> familiarity bucket is automatically Variant.
1. Easy Collocation: In Collocation, Formula, Partial, or Non-unit unity bucket and transparently understood from constituent words.
2. Beginner Core: Typical of literally the first week of a beginner language course. Very few words achieve this category.
3. Ubiquitous: Highly polysemous high-frequency core content words/phrases.
    - Probably used in idiomatic phrases.
    - Also includes high-frequency function words.
4. Active: Part of a universal active vocabulary. Terms of everyday conversation. Commonly produced verbally in the exact given form.
    Must be the most canonical spelling and phrasing. 
    - Does NOT include acronyms or abbreviations unless they are actually produced verbally as such.
    - Literary/SAT vocabulary that people recognize but don’t say is not Active.
5. Colloquial: Slang or jargon produced in coversation by specific cohorts of people in colloquial contexts.
6. General Knowledge: Well-known enough that it has transcended its niche and generation and become a permanent
    fixture of the global culture.
    - Referenced in allusions, metaphors, and/or memes.
    - Makes for easy trivia. Harder trivia belongs in Niche.
7. Inferred: Belongs to a niche, but the meaning is easily inferred from the constituent words.
    - Includes uncommon word inflections that nonetheless are easily understood.
8. Niche: Well-known and belongs to a specific subculture, generational cohort, hobby area, trade, etc.
    - Might make a good difficult trivia answer.
9. Obscure: Not well-known. The information that exists on it may be archaic, technical, and/or hyper-niche.
    - Only people specialized in the area are likely to know it.
    - Might be a non-standard spelling or phrase formulation.
    - Might only exist in another language.
10. Barely Exists: Almost non-existent in the language. Information on it is fragmented.
11. Nonsense: Everything else.

OUTPUT
Output according to the following format, one item per line. Don't include any explanatory or auxiliary text.

<input item> : <bucket name>

WORDS/PHRASES

[[DATA]]
