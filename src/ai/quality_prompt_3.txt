TASK
You are given a list of words and phrases.
Your task is to classify each item into buckets according to its linguistic unity.

TASK
Evaluate and score the linguistic quality, cultural resonance, and phonetic appeal of [[LANG]] words and phrases on a scale from 0.0 to 5.0.

How to score in practice:
The baseline score is a 3 and the score deviates from there based on the following factors. Positive and negative factors can cancel each other out.

GUIDELINES
Positive Drivers (add ~0.5 points each):
• Sparks Joy: Makes people happy upon seeing it.
• High Evocativeness: Instantly conjures rich imagery, sensory details, humor, or deep nostalgia (e.g., campfire, plot twist).
• Conversation Sparker: Highly likely to provoke opinions, stories, or cultural discussions.
• Phonetic/Aesthetic Appeal: Fun to pronounce, musically satisfying, or possesses a unique structural look (e.g., shenanigans, pizzazz, mamby-pamby).
• Intellectual Stimulation: Fascinating enough to inspire someone to look up its etymology or history.

Negative Drivers (subtract ~0.5 points each)
• Vulgarity: Contains profanity, crude slang, or highly offensive terms. Negative connotations don't lower the score, but vulgarity does.
• Awkward Derivation: A derived word variant that is significantly less natural or common than its root (e.g., using slier instead of more sly).
• Shortcuts: Truncations, acronyms, abbreviations, or contractions that are awkward or unnatural compared to alternative forms (e.g., "ctr." instead of "center").
• Clunky Phrasing: The phrasing is noticeably worst than a better alternative. (e.g. "water sip" instead of "sip of water")
• Fragmented/Partial Phrasing: An incomplete segment of a much more common full phrase that is not a common phrasal or modal form. (a lark, run of)
• Unofficial Phrasing: A proper noun or technical term that is not phrased according to its official title (e.g. "Paris Catacombs" instead of "Catacombs of Paris")

GUIDELINES
- Consider the EXACT string given and not a similar one.
- Verify before demoting to Non-unit or Nonsense. 
   Perform a mandatory search to check whether the exact string is indeed used as a Concept, Collocation or Formula, perhaps in a sense that wasn't apparent at first.
   Look for encyclopedia headwords, Reddit threads, forums, articles, etc.
   Try interpreting it as a proper name, especially as a place name, song/movie/book title, etc.
- Non-canonical numbers/ordinals should be rated as Non-unit unless they have other meanings (e.g. "fifty-six" is Non-unit, "eighty-six" is Concept)
- Non-canonical forms of an otherwise unified term → Non-unit (not Concept, Collocation, or Formula), but still verify to double check.
   Includes: wrong word order, nonstandard naming, scrambled variants, superfluous padding, and truncations.
- Plural surnames should be rated as Concept.
- If multiple senses exist, pick the most conventionalized lexicalized one.
- Crossword-style compounds should not be demoted just for non-standard casing or lack of spaces/hyphens, but they are still subject to all other rules.

IMPORTANT: Evaluate the buckets one at a time, starting at the top. Choose the first bucket that applies and then move on.
