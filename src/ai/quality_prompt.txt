TASK
Evaluate and score the linguistic quality, cultural resonance, and phonetic appeal of [[LANG]] words and phrases on a scale from 0.0 to 5.0.

How to score in practice:
The baseline score is a 3 and the score deviates from there based on the following factors. Positive and negative factors can cancel each other out.

GUIDELINES
Positive Drivers (add ~0.5 points each):
• Sparks Joy: Makes people happy upon seeing it.
• High Evocativeness: Instantly conjures rich imagery, sensory details, humor, or deep nostalgia (e.g., campfire, plot twist).
• Conversation Sparker: Highly likely to provoke opinions, stories, or cultural discussions.
• Phonetic/Aesthetic Appeal: Fun to pronounce, musically satisfying, or possesses a unique structural look (e.g., shenanigans, pizzazz, mamby-pamby).
• Intellectual Stimulation: Fascinating enough to inspire someone to look up its etymology or history.

Negative Drivers (subtract ~0.5 points each)
• Vulgarity: Contains profanity, crude slang, or highly offensive terms. Negative connotations don't lower the score, but vulgarity does.
• Awkward Derivation: A derived word variant that is significantly less natural or common than its root (e.g., using slier instead of more sly).
• Shortcuts: Truncations, acronyms, abbreviations, or contractions that are awkward or unnatural compared to alternative forms (e.g., "ctr." instead of "center").
• Clunky Phrasing: The phrasing is noticeably worst than a better alternative. (e.g. "water sip" instead of "sip of water")
• Fragmented/Partial Phrasing: An incomplete segment of a much more common full phrase that is not a common phrasal or modal form. (a lark, run of)
• Unofficial Phrasing: A proper noun or technical term that is not phrased according to its official title (e.g. "Paris Catacombs" instead of "Catacombs of Paris")

RUBRIC
5: Iconic - Linguistically flawless and universally recognized phrases that capture an exact cultural mood, emotion, or vivid image. It's hard to achieve this level.
Examples: save the date, trick or treat, you had me at hello, the American dream, once upon a time

4.5: Beloved - Highly evocative, structurally satisfying, or culturally resonant terms that spark distinct positive associations.
Examples: brainchild, plot twist, comfort food, sibling rivalry, game on!, bromance, brain freeze, mic drop

4: Warm - Familiar, pleasant, and highly functional words or phrases slightly elevated above standard prose.
Examples: cozy, high five, skyscraper, double-check, wedding dress, dill pickle

3.5 Interesting - Contains at least one unique phonetic or conceptual element. Satisfying to encounter, though neutral in emotional weight.
Examples: hadron, sashay, soufflé, wanderlust, quirk, peak TV, kaleidoscope

3: Nothing special (The Baseline) - Standard, functional language. Possesses no notable positive charm, nor any structural flaws or awkwardness.
Examples: document, sidewalk, employee, law, orca, cash register, unit, MBA, NATO, Abel

2.5: Minor Defect (1 Negative Quality) - Slightly awkward, vulgar, or uninspiring.
Examples: Alp (singular is rare), b-day (lazy truncation), disused (stiff alternative to "unused"), booby hatch (slightly vulgar), oft (stiff and archaic)

2: Moderate Defect (2 Negative Qualities) - Noticeably clunky. Combines multiple issues.
Examples: OR docs (worse than "surgeons" + awkward plural), o-neg (obscure short form of O-negative)

1.5: Severe Defect (3+ Negative Qualities) - Highly unnatural, fragmented, or archaic phrasing. Heavily compromised by multiple structural or contextual flaws.
Examples: dept head guy (uncommon abbreviation + clunky alternative + casual phrasing)

1 Barely Coherent - Syntactically mangled or severely broken fragments that technically exist but sound alien or incorrect to a native speaker.
Examples: O woe, Can't I?, went and

0.5: Unused - Anything below 1 scores a 0.

0: Invalid - Misspellings, gibberish, incomprehensible input
Examples: existant (misspelling), xhy (gibberish), greenery paint (incomprehensible)


OUTPUT
Provide your evaluation strictly using the format below. Do not include any introductory, explanatory, or auxiliary text outside of this format.

<original word/phrase> : <quality score>
